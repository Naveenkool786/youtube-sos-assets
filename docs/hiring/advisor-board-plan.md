## 1. Why Advisors Matter More Than You Think

Right now you're one person with a blueprint, a prototype, and a to-do list. Your biggest constraint isn't money — it's **access and credibility**:

- You can't get warm intros to Zara's VP of Quality without someone who knows them
- You can't credibly discuss AQL sampling with ETQ ex-execs without domain language
- You can't get seed VCs to say yes without a trusted name on the pitch deck
- You can't hire a great CTO without someone validating your opportunity to candidates

**One great advisor = 10× productivity on these problems.** They cost you 0.1-0.5% equity + 1-2 hours/month. Best ROI in startup building.

### What advisors give you
1. **Warm intros** — to customers, investors, hires, partners
2. **Domain expertise** — industry nuance you'll take 2 years to learn
3. **Credibility** — their name on your deck validates the opportunity
4. **Pattern recognition** — "I've seen this before" when you're stuck
5. **Honest feedback** — unlike co-founders, they're not emotionally invested
6. **Discipline** — monthly checkins force you to make progress

### What advisors DON'T give you
- Operational execution (they're not your team)
- Daily decisions (they're not your co-founder)
- Fundraising guarantees (they can intro, not promise)
- Free unlimited time (respect the 1-2 hr/month commitment)

**Who they are:** Someone who's built a company from 10 to 100+ people.

**Target profiles:**
- Ex-Head of People at a Series B+ startup

## 3. Equity + Compensation Structure

### Standard advisor equity (industry benchmark)

| Involvement Level | Typical Equity Grant | Time Commitment |
|---|---|---|
| **Light** (quarterly chat, warm intros) | 0.1 – 0.25% | 2 hrs/quarter |
| **Standard** (monthly 1-hr call, intros) | 0.25 – 0.5% | 1 hr/month |
| **Deep** (biweekly, strategic involvement) | 0.5 – 1.0% | 4-8 hrs/month |
| **Marquee** (highly recognized name, deep involvement) | 1.0 – 2.0% | 4-8 hrs/month |

### Vesting
- **2-year vest, monthly cliff** (standard FAST template default)
- 1-year cliff is UNUSUAL for advisors — they're providing value from day 1
- Acceleration on acquisition/exit (double-trigger)

### Cash
- **Default: $0 cash** for seed-stage advisors
- Equity-only is standard
- Exception: if advisor wants $500-$2k/month cash as a "retainer" and brings huge value, consider

### Total advisor pool budget
Reserve **~2.5% total equity** for 5 advisors (avg 0.5% each). Comes out of founder/common equity pre-seed.

### Compliance notes
- Use **Founder Institute's FAST template** (Founder Advisor Standard Template) — free, standardized
- https://fi.co/fast
- Covers vesting, confidentiality, IP, no-conflict
- Have a lawyer review your first FAST ($200-500), then reuse

## 6. First Meeting Agenda (30 minutes)

### Opening (5 min)
- Brief intro of yourself (2-3 lines)
- Walk through what you're building (90 seconds max)
- What you're asking from them (be specific)

### Deep dive (15 min)
- Walk them through pitch deck highlights (not all 12 slides)
- Share the prototype
- Discuss YOUR challenges — not just pitch
- Ask them: "What's your reaction?" and listen hard

### Mutual fit (5 min)
- Ask what they'd want from an advisor relationship
- Discuss time commitment, communication cadence
- Ask about their current advisor commitments

### Close (5 min)
- "Would this be a fit for you?"
- If yes: discuss FAST terms + next steps
- If no: thank them, ask for intros to others who'd fit

## 7. The FAST Agreement — What's In It

Founder Institute's FAST template is the industry standard. Download free: https://fi.co/fast

### Key sections
1. **Services** — What the advisor will do (strategic input, intros, feedback)
2. **Compensation** — Equity %, vesting schedule, cliff (none for advisors)
3. **Confidentiality** — Standard NDA clauses
4. **IP assignment** — Anything they create for you belongs to the company
5. **No conflicts** — Can't advise directly competing companies
6. **Term** — Usually 2 years, auto-renew or terminate
7. **Termination** — Either party can terminate with notice; vested equity remains

### What to customize
- Equity %
- Specific commitments (hours/month, response time expectations)
- Exclusivity scope (narrow to apparel QMS competitors, not all SaaS)

### Legal review
- Have a startup lawyer review your first FAST ($200-500)
- Reuse the modified template for subsequent advisors

## 8. How to Use Your Advisors Well

### Monthly cadence
- 30-60 min call, same time each month
- You drive the agenda — don't waste their time
- 3 topics max per call

### Pre-call prep (always send 24 hours ahead)
> **Agenda for our [date] call:**
> 1. Update on progress since last call (5 min)
> 2. Problem I need your help with: [specific issue] (15 min)
> 3. Ask: can you intro me to [specific people]? (5 min)

### What to ask advisors FOR
- **Specific intros** (not "anyone you know" — specific names)
- **Specific reactions** (to a deck, a hire, a pricing decision)
- **Specific decisions** ("A or B, which do you choose and why?")
- **Pattern recognition** ("have you seen this before?")

### What NOT to ask advisors for
- ❌ Operational execution ("can you make this call for me?")
- ❌ Daily decisions ("should I send this email?")
- ❌ Vendor management ("can you deal with this consultant?")
- ❌ Fundraising guarantees ("will your friend invest?")

### Thank them properly
- Public shout-outs when they help
- Equity vesting monthly (automatic reward)
- End-of-year gift ($100-500 range, personal, thoughtful)
- Invite to launch parties, offsites, team dinners

### Firing an advisor (gently)
If an advisor isn't delivering:
- Stop scheduling monthlies
- They'll naturally fade
- Unvested equity returns to pool after 90 days of no engagement (per FAST default)
- Only have a direct conversation if they ask

## 9. Red Flags When Evaluating Advisors

### Walk away if you see...

- ❌ **Asks for 2%+ equity** without being a marquee name — not aligned
- ❌ **Won't commit to monthly cadence** — too busy to really help
- ❌ **Already on 10+ advisor boards** — spread too thin
- ❌ **No specific intros to offer** on first call — probably won't deliver
- ❌ **Wants cash retainer upfront** without proof of value
- ❌ **Advises competing companies** — potential conflict
- ❌ **Dominates conversation, won't listen** — bad fit for strategic partner
- ❌ **Promises specific investor commitments** — overselling, dishonest signal
- ❌ **Full-time job doesn't allow advising** per their employer contract
- ❌ **Doesn't understand the industry** but is pushing to join anyway

### Green flags

- ✅ **Asks probing questions** before saying yes
- ✅ **Offers specific intros within 48 hours** of first call
- ✅ **Has advised ≤5 companies currently**
- ✅ **Pushes back on your assumptions** respectfully
- ✅ **Has specific domain experience** matching your needs
- ✅ **Treats first call as mutual-fit, not sales**
- ✅ **Willing to be a reference** on your pitch deck
- ✅ **Communicates clearly** on scope, time, equity

## 10. 3-Month Recruitment Sprint

### Month 1: Target List + Outreach
**Week 1:**
- Build spreadsheet of 50 target advisor candidates (10 per type)
- Prioritize warm intro paths (do you know anyone who knows them?)
- Draft outreach templates (A, B, C)

**Week 2:**
- 10 warm intro requests to your existing network
- 20 cold outreaches via LinkedIn
- Download FAST template, have lawyer review

**Week 3-4:**
- Follow up on non-responses
- Schedule first calls as they come in
- Keep pipeline flowing

### Month 2: First Calls + Decisions
**Week 5-6:**
- Conduct 5-10 first calls
- Discuss mutual fit, don't rush close
- Decide YES/NO within 72 hours of each call

**Week 7-8:**
- Issue FAST agreements to top 2-3 YES candidates
- Begin working relationships
- First monthly 1:1s scheduled

### Month 3: Scale + Fill Remaining Seats
**Week 9-10:**
- Continue outreach to fill remaining 2-3 seats
- Advisors already signed may refer others
- First monthly calls with signed advisors

**Week 11-12:**
- Target: 5 advisors signed by end of month
- Formal "Advisor Board" announcement internally
- Begin referring to advisors on pitch deck Team slide

### Realistic timeline
- **3 months to 5 signed advisors** = aggressive but doable
- **6 months is normal** — don't rush bad fits
- **Perfect fits take longer** — be patient on the marquee seat

## 13. Common Advisor Recruitment Mistakes

1. ❌ **Signing anyone who says yes** — bad advisors are worse than no advisors
2. ❌ **Over-indexing on logo advisors** — famous name without engagement is useless
3. ❌ **Giving too much equity upfront** — reserve space for better future advisors
4. ❌ **No clear ask** — "will you be my advisor?" vs "I need help with [X] — would you join to help with that specifically?"
5. ❌ **Letting them ghost** — if no engagement in 90 days, formally offboard
6. ❌ **Treating them as mentors (free)** — they're giving you equity-grade value, respect their time
7. ❌ **Not asking for intros** — biggest wasted opportunity
8. ❌ **Not preparing for meetings** — their 1 hour/month is precious
9. ❌ **Keeping them in the dark** — monthly update email keeps them engaged
10. ❌ **Only recruiting people you already know** — true advisor value comes from EXPANDING your network

## 15. The Honest Truth About Advisors

**Great advisors are hard to find.** You'll contact 20 to sign 5. Of those 5, maybe 2 will be truly transformative. The other 3 will be useful but modest contributors.

**That's OK.** Even 1 transformative advisor justifies the entire effort. The industry-domain expert who introduces you to your anchor customer is worth their equity 100× over.

**Patience > speed.** It's better to run 6 months with 2 great advisors than 3 months with 5 mediocre ones. You can always add more.

**Equity you give them is paid back many times** if you choose well. Don't be stingy with the right people — but be ruthless about not giving equity to the wrong ones.
