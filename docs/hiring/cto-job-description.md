## 1. Why This Role Matters

You will be **employee #1** and the technical foundation of a company building the next-generation Quality Management System for the apparel supply chain — a $1.5T industry that still runs on email, Excel, and WhatsApp.

The product blueprint (190-entity data model, 12 locked technical topics, 17-FTE team plan, 4-region multi-tenant SaaS) is already finalized. A working clickable prototype is live. A seed round is being raised.

**What's missing is the technical leader who turns the blueprint into shipping product and the first team.**

This is a co-founder-grade opportunity. You will:
- Own the entire technical architecture end-to-end
- Hire and lead the first 8–10 engineers over 18 months
- Make build-vs-buy decisions across billing, SSO, data warehouse, integrations
- Deploy a mobile-offline, multi-region, enterprise-ready platform
- Partner with the CEO on fundraising, product strategy, GTM
- Shape the engineering culture permanently

If you've been waiting for a chance to build a systems-of-record SaaS for a huge industry that nobody has gotten right yet — this is that chance.

## 11. Interview Process (5 stages, ~4 weeks)

### Stage 1 — Intro Call (30 min, Founder)
- Mutual fit: are we both excited?
- Share blueprint + prototype under light NDA
- Discuss timing, equity vs cash preference
- **Pass criteria:** Both parties want next conversation

### Stage 2 — Technical Deep-Dive (90 min, Founder + Technical Advisor)
- Walk through the blueprint; ask candidate to critique it
- Discuss their technical philosophy on 3 topics: multi-tenancy, mobile offline, scaling strategy
- No whiteboard coding — discussion of tradeoffs instead
- **Pass criteria:** Pushes back thoughtfully; respects what's good; identifies real risks

### Stage 3 — Working Session (3 hours, paid)
- Candidate reviews a specific sub-problem (e.g., "design the inspection booking state machine") and comes back with a 30-min presentation
- Compensated at $500 flat
- **Pass criteria:** Clear thinking, communicates well, decisions defensible

### Stage 4 — Reference Checks (5–7 refs, back-channeled)
- Prior managers (what was it like to work for them?)
- Prior reports (did people want to stay on their team?)
- Prior peers (how do they handle disagreement?)
- Prior customers (if applicable)
- **Pass criteria:** No red flags on humanity, consistency on strengths

### Stage 5 — Partner Day (full day, in person if possible)
- Half-day working together on real problem
- Half-day business/strategy/vision alignment
- Dinner with CEO + spouse/partner if local
- **Pass criteria:** You both want to co-found a company

### Stage 6 — Offer + Legal
- Written offer with equity, salary, vesting
- 72 hours to decide
- Draft co-founder agreement (vesting, IP, decision rights, exit terms)
- Legal review by startup lawyer

## 13. Red Flags

Walk away or dig deeper if you see:

- ❌ **"I don't do hands-on code anymore."** — Seed-stage CTO must code for first 12+ months.
- ❌ **Weak explanations of tradeoffs.** "We used Kubernetes because it's industry standard" without reasoning.
- ❌ **Can't name 3 past hires they're proud of.** Hiring is 50% of the job.
- ❌ **Only motivated by title / salary.** Red flag for a founder role.
- ❌ **Dismissive about the domain.** "QA is boring, but the tech is cool." Will quit at the first setback.
- ❌ **No independent references willing to speak frankly.** Red flag for interpersonal issues.
- ❌ **Visa / work authorization issues** that can't be resolved in 90 days (U.S. roles).
- ❌ **Non-compete from prior employer** that would restrict first 12 months.
- ❌ **Over-indexes on fancy tech** (microservices, Kubernetes, GraphQL) without rationale.
- ❌ **Vague on prior equity outcomes.** Suggests either dishonesty or lack of experience.
- ❌ **Treats interview process as transactional.** Doesn't ask real questions about vision.
