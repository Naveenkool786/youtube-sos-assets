## 1. The Fundamental Question

Where you incorporate determines:
- **How investors see you** (and whether they can invest at all)
- **Where you pay taxes** (and how much)
- **How you can hire employees** (and give them equity)
- **How you can be acquired or IPO** (and what tax hit you take)
- **How much ongoing compliance cost** you bear ($5k/year vs $50k/year)

**Pick wrong and you'll likely need to "flip" entities later** — a $20–50k operation with 6-month timeline that most founders regret.

---

## 2. The 3 Main Options

### Option A: Delaware C-Corporation (US)
**The default for VC-backed startups globally.** Even founders in India, Singapore, Europe end up here when raising US capital.

### Option B: India Private Limited Company
**The natural choice for Indian founders.** Simple to set up, but creates friction with US/global VCs.

### Option C: Singapore Private Limited Company (Pte Ltd)
**The APAC-friendly middle ground.** Low tax, investor-friendly, closer to India for operations.

### Plus 2 Hybrid Structures (Common for Indian Founders)
- **Delaware C-Corp + India Subsidiary** — US holding company owns India operating entity
- **Singapore Pte Ltd + India Subsidiary** — Singapore holding company owns India operating entity

## 6. The "Delaware Flip" Structure Explained

Most Indian founders end up with this structure. Here's how it works:

```
                   ┌────────────────────────────┐
                   │  Delaware C-Corp (Parent)   │
                   │  - You own ~85% as founder  │
                   │  - Investors own ~15%       │
                   │  - Issues equity to all     │
                   │  - US bank account (Mercury)│
                   │  - Signs enterprise deals   │
                   │  - Collects revenue in USD  │
                   └──────────────┬──────────────┘
                                  │ 100% owned
                                  ▼
                   ┌────────────────────────────┐
                   │  [Company] India Pvt Ltd    │
                   │  - 100% subsidiary of DE    │
                   │  - Employs India team       │
                   │  - Pays India payroll       │
                   │  - Operates on INR cost+15% │
                   │    transfer pricing margin  │
                   └────────────────────────────┘
```

### Transfer pricing (the critical mechanics)

- India subsidiary provides services (engineering) to Delaware parent
- Charges parent at "cost + 10-15%" (standard arm's length)
- India subsidiary pays Indian corporate tax (25%+) on ~15% margin
- Delaware parent books majority revenue; pays US corporate tax on US profits
- Must have a proper **Transfer Pricing Study** done annually (~₹100k)

### Who signs what
- **Customer contracts:** Delaware parent signs (US paper)
- **Employee contracts:** India subsidiary signs for India employees
- **Vendor contracts:** Either, based on business need
- **IP ownership:** Delaware parent owns all IP (via Services Agreement with India sub)

### Fundraising
- Investors invest in **Delaware parent** (not India sub)
- Equity flows to founders through Delaware (not India)
- Simple cap table at parent level

## 9. QSBS — The Hidden Delaware Advantage

**Qualified Small Business Stock exclusion** = IRS Section 1202.

### What it is
- Hold Delaware C-Corp stock for **5+ years**
- Up to **$10M per founder** (or 10× basis, whichever is greater) in capital gains at exit = **tax-free**
- At current rates: saves founder **$2-3M in federal tax** on a $10M exit

### Requirements
- C-Corp (not LLC or S-Corp)
- Issued after 2010 (easy)
- Company gross assets ≤ $50M when stock issued (easy for seed)
- Active business (not investment holding)
- Original issuance (not secondary purchase)

### Why it matters NOW
- The clock starts when you ISSUE the stock
- Need to issue stock to founders at formation (not later)
- File **83(b) election within 30 DAYS of stock issuance** — this is critical

### Who gets it
- Only works for US citizens / green card holders at time of sale
- Doesn't apply to non-US founders (unless they become US tax residents by exit)

**If you plan to become US tax resident eventually, QSBS alone can be worth more than everything else combined. Delaware wins just for this.**

## 10. Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. ❌ **Forming an LLC "to save money" early** — LLCs can't receive VC investment cleanly; you'll convert later at cost
2. ❌ **Missing the 30-day 83(b) deadline** — turns ordinary wages into capital gains (massive tax hit)
3. ❌ **Giving founder equity without a vesting schedule** — VCs will force restructuring
4. ❌ **Using parent-child entities without transfer pricing documentation** — IRS/Indian tax authority audit risk
5. ❌ **Choosing India PLC "for now, flip later"** — flipping costs $30-100k and 6 months
6. ❌ **DIY cross-border structure without tax advisor** — one wrong move = PE (Permanent Establishment) problems
7. ❌ **Not using a registered agent** — missed state notices = loss of good standing
8. ❌ **Delaying banking setup** — can't collect revenue, can't pay vendors
9. ❌ **Co-mingling personal and business funds** — pierces corporate veil
10. ❌ **No bylaws / operating agreement** — governance gaps cause co-founder disputes

## 13. Tax Residency Considerations

**This is where mistakes get expensive.** You need to understand YOUR personal tax residency and how it interacts with the entity.

### If you're an Indian citizen living in India
- Your personal income is taxed in India regardless of entity structure
- Capital gains from Delaware stock sale = taxed in India at 20% LTCG + surcharge
- Delaware entity income taxed at corporate rate; your dividend/salary from it = Indian tax
- Benefit: can receive salary from Delaware parent, taxed as foreign income in India

### If you move to the US
- Need L-1 or E-2 visa (or green card)
- Become US tax resident; QSBS kicks in
- Significant tax planning opportunity if timed right
- Need to track "domicile" for state tax too

### If you move to Singapore / UAE / tax-friendly jurisdiction
- Different rules per jurisdiction
- May be able to receive capital gains tax-free
- Needs 182+ days physical presence typically
- Consider pre-exit emigration planning

**Hire a cross-border tax specialist in Year 1.** The $3-5k investment prevents 7-figure tax surprises at exit.

## 14. Equity Compensation by Entity

### Delaware C-Corp
- **Options (ISOs or NSOs):** Standard; typical 10-15% employee pool
- **RSUs:** Available post-Series B typically
- **Early exercise + 83(b):** Standard for founders and early employees
- **QSBS tracking:** Each employee tracks their own 5-year clock

### India Pvt Ltd
- **ESOPs allowed** under Companies Act 2013
- Must create ESOP scheme + get board approval
- Tax at EXERCISE (not grant) — considered perquisite income
- No early exercise mechanism
- Difficult to grant to foreign employees without regulatory approval

### Singapore Pte Ltd
- **ESOPs under Companies Act** (Singapore)
- Simpler than India, more structured than Delaware
- No capital gains tax = favorable
- Can grant to global employees

### Hybrid (DE + IN subsidiary)
- All equity issued from **Delaware parent** (not India subsidiary)
- India employees receive equity in Delaware
- India tax: subject to RBI FEMA rules (needs regulatory approval for shares in foreign company)
- Complex but doable with proper advisor

## 16. When to Flip (If You Picked India First)

If you've already incorporated as India Pvt Ltd and need to flip to Delaware:

### Why to flip
- US VC said yes but requires Delaware
- Series A term sheet made it a condition
- Expanding to US and need US entity for contracts

### Cost
- **$30-80k legal + tax fees**
- **4-6 months process**
- **Potential tax events** for existing shareholders
- **Transfer pricing complications** if India team becomes a subsidiary

### Process (simplified)
1. Form Delaware C-Corp
2. All India shareholders exchange India shares for Delaware shares
3. Delaware entity becomes 100% owner of India entity
4. India entity converts to wholly-owned subsidiary
5. Full RBI, FEMA, SEBI, income tax approvals needed
6. Transfer pricing study + Services Agreement finalized
7. Ongoing compliance in both entities

### Best time to flip
- Before Series A (easier)
- When you have ~5-10 employees (less complex than later)
- Before significant IP has been created in India entity

**Lesson:** If you have any US ambitions, pay the extra $5-10k now to do Delaware + India from day 1. Avoid the flip.
