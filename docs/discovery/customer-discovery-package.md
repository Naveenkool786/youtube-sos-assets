## 1. Why Customer Discovery Matters MORE Than You Think

Your blueprint has three load-bearing assumptions that MUST be validated by real customers before you spend $5M of seed money:

1. **Brand QA heads will champion a new QMS to replace email + Excel** (not just complain about it)
2. **Factories will adopt a Brand-mandated tool without resistance** (critical for the Brand-sponsored model)
3. **3P inspectors will pay per-inspection fees** to use your platform instead of their current tools

**If any of these fail in interviews, you must adjust the blueprint before writing code.**

Great customer discovery:
- Gets you from "I think this is a problem" → "I've heard 15 people describe this specific pain"
- Surfaces unknown pains your blueprint missed
- Builds a warm pipeline (every interviewee is a potential anchor customer)
- Gives you direct quotes for the pitch deck ("Our Head of QA at [Brand] said...")
- Prevents 6-month wasted builds of features nobody wants

**Investors always ask: "How many customers have you talked to?"** You need a credible answer — ideally 20+ by seed close.

## 2. The Mom Test Framework (Rob Fitzpatrick)

Three rules that separate good from bad customer interviews:

### Rule 1: Talk about their life, not your idea
❌ "Would you use a QMS that connects brands, factories, and 3P inspectors?"
✅ "Walk me through the last quality issue you had — from first notice to resolution."

### Rule 2: Ask about specifics in the past, not generics or opinions about the future
❌ "Would you pay $2,500/month for a tool like this?"
✅ "What tools did you buy last year, and what did they cost?"

### Rule 3: Talk less, listen more
Target ratio: **80% them talking, 20% you talking.** If you catch yourself pitching, stop immediately.

### Compliments are worthless signal
If someone says "that sounds great," you learned nothing. If they say "I spent 4 hours yesterday on this exact problem" — that's gold.

### Commitments and advances are the currency
Good interview outcomes aren't "they liked it" — they're:
- "Introduce me to [specific person]"
- "I'll beta test when ready"
- "Here's my calendar for follow-up"
- "What's the price?"

## 3. Three Target Personas

Discovery requires talking to all three. Prioritize Brands first (they're the sponsors), then Factories (adoption gates), then 3Ps (nice-to-have).

### Persona 1 — BRAND QA DIRECTOR (highest priority)

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Titles to search | Head of Quality, QA Director, VP Quality, Director of Supply Chain Quality, Compliance Director |
| Company size | $100M – $5B revenue (mid-market sweet spot); $5B+ for enterprise cohort |
| Industries | Apparel, fashion, footwear, outdoor, athleisure, home textiles |
| Where they sit | Usually under COO or VP Supply Chain |
| Primary pains | Shipment-stop defects, late discovery of problems, factory audit coordination, CoQ reporting to board |
| Buying authority | Sponsors or decides on tools up to ~$50k/yr; needs CFO signoff above |
| Where they hang out | LinkedIn, AAFA events, Sourcing at MAGIC trade shows, Just-Style forums |

**Interview goals:**
- Validate severity of pain (1–10 scale)
- Understand current workaround cost (hours, dollars)
- Learn their buying process (who signs, budget cycle, procurement)
- Get price elasticity ("you've paid $X for Y — what would you pay for Z?")
- Secure LOI or pilot commitment if interest is strong

### Persona 2 — FACTORY GENERAL MANAGER / QUALITY HEAD

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Titles to search | General Manager, Plant Head, Factory Director, Quality Manager, QA Head |
| Company size | 500–5,000 workers (sweet spot) |
| Country focus | Bangladesh, Vietnam, India, Türkiye, China (Dhaka, HCMC, Tirupur, Istanbul, Guangzhou) |
| Primary pains | Brand compliance burden, duplicate inspection reporting, rework costs, audit prep |
| Buying authority | Usually $0 (Brand pays); will advocate upward if value is clear |
| Where they hang out | BGMEA (Bangladesh), VITAS (Vietnam), AEPC (India), industry WhatsApp groups |

**Interview goals:**
- Validate "Brand-sponsored, free to Factory" model acceptance
- Understand what features Factory would ADD to a Brand-provided tool
- Learn resistance patterns (what would make them refuse to use it?)
- Measure digital readiness (what tools do they use today?)

### Persona 3 — 3rd PARTY INSPECTION OPERATIONS MANAGER

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Titles to search | Operations Manager, Country Manager, General Manager (inspection firms) |
| Companies | SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek, TÜV, QIMA — and 50+ mid-size regional firms |
| Primary pains | Inspector scheduling complexity, report standardization across clients, revenue per inspection |
| Buying authority | Variable — $500–$5k/mo tool budget without exec approval |
| Where they hang out | LinkedIn, inspection industry forums, Inspection Journal, SGS/BV internal communities |

**Interview goals:**
- Validate per-inspection pricing model acceptance
- Understand white-label vs branded platform preferences
- Learn integration needs with their existing LIMS/CRM
- Discover: do they want OUR platform, or an API into theirs?

## 6. Interview Scripts

### Interview Length
- **Target: 30 minutes** (promise 20; most will go 30–45 if engaged)
- **Opening** (3 min): Thank them, state goal, ask permission to record
- **Current state** (10 min): How things work today
- **Pain discovery** (10 min): What's broken, impact, cost
- **Buying signals** (5 min): Who decides, when, how much
- **Closing** (2 min): Advance request, intros, follow-up

### SCRIPT: Brand QA Director

#### Opening (3 min)

> "Thanks so much for the time. I'm researching quality management in apparel supply chains — specifically how brands like yours coordinate with factories and 3rd-party inspectors. I'm not pitching anything today; I'm trying to understand the real pain points before I decide what to build.
>
> Would it be OK if I record this just for my own notes? I won't share it anywhere without asking."

#### Current State (10 min)

1. "Can you walk me through what your QA team looks like? How many people, and what does each person own?"
2. "How many factories do you work with globally? Rough number is fine."
3. "Of those, how many use 3rd-party inspection firms? Which ones do you use most?"
4. "Walk me through what happens when a new PO goes out — from the design handoff to the factory, through production, inspection, and shipment arrival. What are the main steps?"
5. "What tools and software are you using today for all of this?"

#### Pain Discovery (10 min)

6. "Tell me about the last quality issue that cost you real money — what happened?"
7. "Walk me through how your team found out about it, communicated with the factory, and resolved it."
8. "How long did that process take? And how much did it cost — in rework, in late delivery, in return rate?"
9. "What's the one thing in your QA process you wish you could change tomorrow?"
10. "What have you tried before to fix this? What worked, what didn't?"
11. "On a scale of 1–10, how painful is this problem for your team right now?"
12. "If a magic wand could solve one problem across your supply chain quality, what would it be?"

#### Buying Signals (5 min)

13. "What tools did you buy last year, and what did they cost per year roughly?"
14. "If a tool solved [specific pain they mentioned], what would it be worth to your team?"
15. "Who signs for a new tool at your price range? What's the typical approval cycle?"
16. "What time of year do you plan your tech budget?"

#### Closing (2 min)

17. "If I come back in 2 months with something built, would you be open to seeing it?"
18. "Is there anyone else at [Company] or in your network I should talk to?"
19. "What questions should I be asking that I'm not?"

### SCRIPT: Factory GM / Quality Head

#### Opening (3 min)
Same as above, substitute "factory operations" for "quality management."

#### Current State (10 min)

1. "Tell me about your factory — how many workers, how many lines, what garment types?"
2. "How many brands do you supply? Which are your top 3 by volume?"
3. "How many of them send 3rd-party inspectors? What's the inspection process like?"
4. "Walk me through what happens when a new PO comes in — from brand confirmation to final shipment."
5. "What systems do you use for production tracking, QC, and brand communication? Specific names please."

#### Pain Discovery (10 min)

6. "What's the hardest part about working with Brand audits and 3P inspections?"
7. "Tell me about the last NCR or shipment hold you had — what happened and how did you handle it?"
8. "If a Brand said 'use this new quality platform or we won't do business with you,' how would you react? Honestly."
9. "What would make you say YES immediately to a new quality tool? What would make you say NO?"
10. "How much time per week does your team spend on reporting to brands?"
11. "On a scale of 1–10, how painful is the current inspection/audit process?"

#### Buying Signals (5 min)

12. "If a Brand paid for a tool and you had to use it — what features would you want to use in return?"
13. "If YOU were paying, what would you expect to pay per month for a quality platform? Honestly."
14. "Do you have your own in-house QA software, or do you buy from vendors?"

#### Closing (2 min)

15. "If we build this with brands first and factories get it free, would you champion adoption at your plant?"
16. "Would you be willing to beta test when ready, in exchange for pricing lock?"
17. "Can you introduce me to other factory GMs in your network?"

### SCRIPT: 3P Inspection Ops Manager

#### Opening (3 min)
Same intro.

#### Current State (10 min)

1. "Tell me about your operations — how many inspectors, which countries, which brands?"
2. "What does an inspector's typical day look like, from receiving an assignment to publishing a report?"
3. "What tools do inspectors use in the field? What tools do you use for scheduling?"
4. "How do you deliver inspection reports to brands? Email PDF? Custom portal?"
5. "How many different brand-specific workflows do you have to maintain?"

#### Pain Discovery (10 min)

6. "What's the biggest operational bottleneck for your inspection business right now?"
7. "What % of inspections are delayed or rescheduled, and why?"
8. "How do you handle offline-mobile scenarios? Have you ever lost field data?"
9. "What do your inspectors complain about most?"
10. "Tell me about the last time a brand pushed back on a report — what happened?"

#### Buying Signals (5 min)

11. "What's your current tech stack cost per inspector per year?"
12. "If a platform charged you $3–5 per inspection but handled scheduling + mobile + reporting + brand delivery, would that be a bargain or expensive?"
13. "Would you white-label a platform under your brand, or use ours directly?"

#### Closing (2 min)

14. "Could I shadow one of your inspectors for a day to see the real workflow?"
15. "Would you be interested in piloting a new platform if it solved [top pain]?"

## 7. What to LISTEN FOR — Signal Classification

### 🟢 Strong buying signals
- "We spent $X on this problem last year"
- "I have budget allocated for this"
- "When can we see a demo?"
- "Can you talk to [specific colleague]?"
- "I'll beta test when it's ready"
- Specific stories with specific numbers (hours, dollars, headcount)
- Unprompted expansion of pain beyond your questions

### 🟡 Mild signal (continue but with caution)
- "That sounds interesting"
- "We've thought about building something like that"
- "Our team could probably use it"
- Generic descriptions without numbers
- Deflection to "corporate IT" or "procurement"

### 🔴 No-signal (don't pursue, but learn)
- "That's a nice idea"
- "I'd have to check with my team"
- "Send me more info" (classic brush-off)
- Can't recall specifics when asked
- Interrupts to pitch THEIR solution
- Shifts to feature requests without pain articulation

### ⚠️ Red flags about your blueprint
- Multiple people say "that's not how we work"
- No one has the pain you assumed
- Pricing far outside what you projected
- Factory GMs refuse Brand-mandated tools
- 3Ps already building their own platform

## 8. Interview Scoring Rubric

Score each interview 1–5 on these dimensions. Target: 15+ scoring ≥ 3.5 average.

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pain acknowledgement** | No pain articulated | Mild pain mentioned | Specific recent incident with cost |
| **Workaround cost** | None quantified | Hours or days mentioned | Specific $ or % impact |
| **Buying authority** | Clear no-budget | Can influence budget | Signs or allocates budget |
| **Advance commitment** | None | Agreed to follow-up | Introduction / LOI / pilot |
| **Blueprint validation** | Doesn't match assumptions | Partially matches | Confirms blueprint |

Total score /25 — tag as:
- **22–25:** Hot lead (follow up weekly)
- **17–21:** Warm (monthly check-in)
- **12–16:** Qualified learning (no sales follow-up but preserve relationship)
- **< 12:** Not fit for v1

## 13. Common Discovery Mistakes to Avoid

1. ❌ **Pitching your idea** — you're there to learn, not sell
2. ❌ **Leading questions** — "Don't you hate when...?" biases answers
3. ❌ **Asking about future hypotheticals** — "Would you pay $X?" is worthless
4. ❌ **Not recording** — your memory will fail you by Interview #5
5. ❌ **Skipping synthesis** — raw notes have 10% of the value of synthesized docs
6. ❌ **Only talking to friends** — bias everywhere; need adversarial perspectives
7. ❌ **Rejecting negative signals** — bad news is the most valuable data
8. ❌ **Interviewing too few people** — 5 interviews = anecdotes; 20 = patterns
9. ❌ **Not asking for advances** — every interview should have a close
10. ❌ **Treating customers as monolith** — Brand ≠ Factory ≠ 3P perspectives

## 15. What a Great Interview Sounds Like (Example)

**Bad opening (don't do this):**
> "Hi, I'm building a QMS platform for apparel supply chains that connects brands, factories, and 3rd-party inspectors with mobile-first field inspection, live factory floor twin, and AI-powered defect detection. Would that be useful for you?"

**Good opening:**
> "Thanks for the time. I'm trying to understand how quality issues actually get caught and resolved in apparel supply chains — before anyone's shipped a product. Can you walk me through the last quality incident your team dealt with, start to finish?"

**Bad follow-up:**
> "That sounds hard. Would you use a tool that solved all of that?"

**Good follow-up:**
> "You mentioned 11 days to resolve. Who was involved in that 11 days? What were they doing each day?"

**Great closing:**
