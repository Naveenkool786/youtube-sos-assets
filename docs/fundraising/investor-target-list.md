## 1. Fundraising Strategy — The 90-Day Game

A seed round is NOT a single pitch meeting. It's a **90-day sprint** with specific milestones:

| Phase | Weeks | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1 — Prep** | 1–2 | Materials polished; list built; warm intros requested |
| **Phase 2 — Warm wave** | 3–6 | 20-30 intro meetings; collect feedback; iterate |
| **Phase 3 — Momentum** | 7–10 | Second meetings, partner meetings, due diligence |
| **Phase 4 — Close** | 11–13 | Term sheets, legal, wire |

**Rule of thumb:** 100 investor contacts → 30 first meetings → 10 second meetings → 2-3 term sheets → 1 lead investor.

**Timing:** Fundraising is a full-time job for 60-90 days. Block your calendar.

## 3. Target Investor Universe

For a vertical SaaS + supply chain + apparel seed, 4 investor categories matter:

### Category A: Vertical SaaS Specialists (HIGHEST PRIORITY)
These firms live for your category. They understand the thesis without hand-holding.

### Category B: Supply Chain / Logistics Investors
Sector-adjacent. They've seen Flexport, Project44, FourKites — understand the TAM.

### Category C: Enterprise B2B Generalists
Big Tier-1 firms. Harder to get into, but transformative if won.

### Category D: Angels + Operator Investors
Smaller checks ($25-100k each), but highest ROI for domain + reputation.

Target split for a $5M round:
- 1 lead investor (Vertical SaaS or Supply Chain): $2–3M
- 2-3 participating funds (Category A/B): $500k–$1M each
- 10-20 angels (Category D): $25-100k each → $500k-1M total

## 13. First Meeting Agenda (30 minutes typical)

### Opening (3 min)
- Thanks + brief context on where you are in process
- Ask: "How much do you already know about [us] / [the space]?"

### Pitch (10-15 min)
- Walk through 10-12 slides at ~1 min each
- SLOW DOWN on: Problem, Why Now, Business Model, Team
- SPEED THROUGH: standard SaaS mechanics they already know

### Q&A (10 min)
- Let them drive
- Common questions in Section 14 — have crisp answers

### Close (2 min)
- "What are your next steps?"
- "Who else do I need to meet in your firm?"
- "What's the typical timeline from here?"
- Specific advance ask: "Can I share the full data room?" or "Can we set up a partner meeting?"

### Follow-up
- Send thank-you + materials within 24 hours
- Add to CRM with next action + date

## 15. Red Flags on Investors (Walk Away If You See)

- ❌ **Takes 3+ weeks to schedule first meeting** when you're actively raising
- ❌ **Won't share decision timeline** — leaves you dangling
- ❌ **Pushes for valuation discount >30% from market** without clear justification
- ❌ **Demands board seat at seed stage** (board observer OK; seat for lead only)
- ❌ **Requires 25%+ dilution** on standard seed (abnormal)
- ❌ **No prior investments in your space** AND no interest in learning
- ❌ **Partner you met won't bring to full partnership** (dead-end)
- ❌ **Pattern of slow follow-through** (ask founders in their portfolio)
- ❌ **References from current portfolio founders are tepid** or awkward
- ❌ **Pressures you to decide quickly** on their offer without full-process optionality
- ❌ **Term sheet has unusual provisions** (full ratchet, broad veto rights, aggressive liquidation preferences)
- ❌ **Communicates poorly during diligence** (will be 10× worse post-investment)

### Green flags
- ✅ **Fast initial response** (within 48 hours)
- ✅ **Partner you met owns the decision** (not junior passing up)
- ✅ **Specific questions showing they read the deck**
- ✅ **Connects you with portfolio founders for intros/learnings**
- ✅ **Standard terms** (YC SAFE, standard preferred)
- ✅ **Clear next step after each meeting**
- ✅ **Operator background** (ex-founder/exec, not pure finance)

## 16. Term Sheet Basics (Don't Sign Without Lawyer)

### For a seed round, expect:

| Term | Normal Range (Seed) | Watch Out For |
|---|---|---|
| **Round size** | $3-5M | — |
| **Pre-money valuation** | $15-25M | Lower = more dilution |
| **Post-money valuation** | $20-30M | — |
| **Instrument** | SAFE (post-money) or Convertible Note | Pre-money SAFE = less founder-friendly |
| **Lead investor %** | 50-70% of round | Below 50% = no real lead |
| **Discount (notes)** | 20% | >25% abnormal |
| **Valuation cap** | $20-25M typical | Lower = more dilution if cap breaks |
| **MFN clause** | Standard | Protects early investors |
| **Pro-rata rights** | Standard for lead | Protect dilution next round |
| **Board seat** | Lead gets 1 seat | Any observer rights for others only |
| **Option pool** | 10% expansion pre-$ | More = more founder dilution |
| **Vesting** | 4-year, 1-year cliff for new hires | Founders typically vest 4yr |
| **Information rights** | Standard | — |
| **Protective provisions** | Minimal at seed | Avoid broad vetos |

### Key questions to ask about each term sheet
1. What's the real pre-money (after pool expansion)?
2. Who's on the board?
3. Pro-rata rights for all or just lead?
4. Timeline to close?
5. What's expected after close (board cadence, reporting, etc.)?

### Your BATNA (Best Alternative to Negotiated Agreement)
- Walk-away willingness is leverage
- Multiple term sheets > one term sheet
- Don't share one investor's terms with another ("term sheet shopping")

**Hire a startup lawyer BEFORE signing anything.** Flat fee usually $5-15k for seed round legal work.

## 21. Common Fundraising Mistakes to Avoid

1. ❌ **Starting with Tier 1 Benchmark/Sequoia** — Get warmed up on Tier 2 first; refine pitch
2. ❌ **Running round without a lead** — Participators need a lead to anchor
3. ❌ **Taking first offer because you're scared** — Create competitive dynamics first
4. ❌ **Not sharing data room until Term Sheet** — Slows process; share earlier
5. ❌ **Over-pitching, under-listening** — Great founders ask investors questions
6. ❌ **Letting round drag beyond 90 days** — Momentum dies; signal weakness
7. ❌ **Negotiating alone (no lawyer)** — $5-15k legal fees prevent $500k+ mistakes
8. ❌ **Accepting bad terms for high valuation** — Preference stack, control rights matter more
9. ❌ **Hiding bad news from investors** — They find out; damages trust permanently
10. ❌ **Skipping monthly updates post-close** — Lose follow-on round support

## 22. The Honest Truth About Seed Fundraising

### What you'll hear from investors
- 70% will say no, politely
- 20% will say "maybe later / after traction"
- 10% will say yes

### Your job
Find the 10% who are right-fit. Not every investor is wrong — most just aren't a fit for your category/stage/geography.

### Timing matters
- Q1 and Q3 are strongest fundraising windows
- Summer (Jul-Aug) and holidays (Dec) are dead zones
- Target Q1 2027 close = start outreach January 2027 ideally

### Mental model
- First 10 meetings: You're learning how to pitch
- Meetings 11-30: You're calibrating against feedback
- Meetings 31-50: You're closing
- Beyond 50: You're probably doing something wrong — rethink positioning

### What investors are REALLY buying
Not the deck. Not the metrics. **You.**
- Can YOU attract talent?
- Can YOU sell to Enterprise?
- Can YOU weather 10 nos a day for 3 months?
- Can YOU become the CEO this company needs?
